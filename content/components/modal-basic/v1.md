Modals are temporary overlays used for important tasks like confirmations or forms. They must include a clear title, concise content, and actions, with easy ways to close (button, Esc key, or tapping outside). Modals should trap focus for accessibility and use smooth animations. On mobile, they adapt to full-width or slide-up styles.

## Anatomy

- The anatomy includes a header with a clear title and a close button, a body section with a heading and supporting text, and a footer holding the primary and secondary action buttons (Cancel and Confirm). The whole modal sits in a central container over a dimmed background overlay.
- 1. Dialog Title, 2. Section Heading, 3. Dialog Body/Content, 4. Cancel Button, 5. Close Icon, 6. Dialog Separator, 7. Scrollbar, 8. Modal component, 9. Primary Action Button (Confirm)

## Size

- All modals use a max width of 600px, not 320px, to support readability and layout flexibility
- Modal spacing (tokens): title top padding spacing/md; title-to-heading spacing/md; heading top margin spacing/md; heading-to-body spacing/lg; body bottom padding spacing/md; button area top margin spacing/md; button area bottom padding spacing/md; left/right modal padding spacing/md; button horizontal gap spacing/sm; right button padding spacing/md
- Modal buttons: padding top/bottom spacing/sm, padding left/right spacing/md
- Modal forms: heading top padding spacing/md; heading-to-label spacing/md; option-to-option spacing/md; form-fields-to-paragraph spacing/md; bottom/right modal padding spacing/md; option padding top/bottom spacing/sm, left spacing/sm; checkbox-to-option-text gap spacing/sm

## Behaviour

- Modals appear in response to a user action and take focus immediately on opening. While open, interaction is limited to the modal — background content is dimmed and inaccessible via a backdrop and focus trap. Dismissible via a visible close button and Escape, unless a critical task requires completion. Focus returns to the triggering element on close. Modals should not stack, and transitions should be smooth and fast.
- If showing critical information, provide an "x" to close. If a critical user response is required, don't include the "x" — the user should make an input action, either "Cancel" or "Confirm".

## Types

**Modal/warning** — Used to alert users to critical or potentially destructive actions (e.g. deleting an item, exiting without saving). Clear, concise messaging with strong visual hierarchy — often an attention-grabbing icon or colour. Include a descriptive title, an explanation of the consequence, and clear action buttons — typically a primary (e.g. "Delete") and a secondary (e.g. "Cancel"). Must be easily dismissible and accessible.

**Modal/scrollable** — For longer content (policy information, product details, terms and conditions) without overwhelming screen space. Vertical scrolling should be enabled within the modal body, not the background. Title stays fixed at the top; action buttons remain visible or easily accessible.

**Modal/forms** — For short, focused user input tasks. Structured with a clear heading, form labels, fields, and submit/cancel buttons. Use inline validation and helpful error messaging. All form elements properly labelled and accessible with a logical tab order.

## Accessibility

- Properly announced by screen readers, with focus trapped inside while open and dismissible via Escape
- Use semantic HTML (&lt;dialog&gt; or &lt;div&gt; with role="dialog"), label with aria-labelledby and aria-describedby, and keep a logical focus order
- Don't rely solely on visual cues — include clear labels and instructions, and give keyboard-only users full control to open, navigate, and close

## Content guidelines

**Start with a clear, specific title** — e.g. "Delete Item", not a vague "Alert" or "Attention".

**Write purpose-driven body content** — Explain what's happening, why it matters, and what's expected. e.g. "Are you sure you want to permanently delete this file? This action cannot be undone."

**Use clear, action-oriented button labels** — "Delete", "Save Changes", "Continue Without Saving" — not "OK" or "Yes".

**Support cancellation or dismissal** — Always provide a secondary button ("Cancel"/"Back") and explain the effect of cancelling if progress would be lost.

**Use supporting text for context or warnings** — For irreversible actions, add a brief supporting paragraph; use bold/colour sparingly to highlight warnings.

**Be concise, especially on small screens** — A few sentences at most — avoid decorative language or lengthy terms and conditions.

**Maintain a logical content hierarchy** — Title, then supporting text, then actions — most important information first.

**Avoid overloading with too many actions** — Two or three options maximum, usually a primary and a secondary button.

**Match tone to context** — Calm and neutral for standard interactions, direct for urgent alerts, friendly for onboarding tips.

**Use inline links or tooltips for additional info** — Keep the core message clean; put help text or legal references behind an optional link/tooltip.
