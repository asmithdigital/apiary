Buttons on a website serve as interactive elements that enable users to perform actions or navigate through the site. They are a crucial component of a website's user interface and user experience, providing clear and intuitive means for interaction.

## Variants

- Primary, secondary, icon left/right, icon only, text, selected, error, details, back-to-top, loading

## Anatomy

- The anatomy of a button contains a background colour, border, label, and optional icon. The icon may be on the left or right of the label, or it may be an icon-only button depending on context. The border, label, icon, and background surface depend on the button type and interaction state.
- 1. Border, 2. Background surface, 3. Label, 4. Icon

## Size

- Margin top and bottom — 12px
- Margin left and right — 24px
- Gap — 12px

## Typography

- Label — National 2 Bold/14px | 20px, P2/100
- Icon — Font Awesome 6 Pro/14px | 20px, P2/100

## States

- Default, hover, active, focus, disabled
- Feedback states: loading, error, or selected/success — used to reflect the interface's status after a click

## Transitions

- Loading transition hides the label during the loading animation

## Behaviour

- Loading transition hides the label during the loading animation

## Types

**Primary** — Used for the most important call to action. States: Default, Hover, Focus, Active.

**Secondary** — Used for secondary actions.

**Icon buttons** — Icons are used to inform the functionality of the button, not for decoration.

## Arrangement & spacing

- Align buttons right/left in modals and drawers
- Align to the left in page navigation

## Accessibility

- Using two primary buttons together can cause confusion and dilute the call to action — use one primary button for the most critical action, and a secondary or link style for less critical ones, to maintain clear visual hierarchy
- Ensure buttons are accessible to screen reader users — use aria-labels if the button text alone doesn't provide enough context
- Avoid disabling buttons — instead allow the interaction and show an error message with instructions on how to proceed. (Source notes disabled buttons may still be appropriate during an active loading animation — open question.)

## Do

- Use primary and secondary/text together
- Use an icon left/right or icon only
- Use one icon per button

## Don't

- Use multiple primary buttons together
- Place an icon both left and right
- Allow the label to wrap to multiple lines
- Use multiple icons in a single button

## Content guidelines

**Use clear, actionable, and concise language** — Precisely convey the action taken on click; keep text to roughly one to three words so it fits and stays readable. Start with a verb ("Submit", "Download", "Buy Now", "Learn More"). Avoid technical jargon.

**Be consistent** — Maintain consistent terminology, capitalisation, and verb forms across buttons, plus consistent colour and shape, so users recognise buttons easily.

**Consider hierarchy** — Use size, colour, or placement to prioritise buttons when there are several on a page — a primary and secondary pairing establishes clear hierarchy.

**Provide feedback** — Give immediate feedback on click — colour/shape change or a loading animation — to confirm the action is processing. Use hover effects to signal clickability.
