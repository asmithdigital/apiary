Banners highlight important messages at the top of a page. They use clear icons, colours, and concise text to communicate status or actions, such as information, warnings, or success. Banners can include links or buttons and may be dismissible.

## Anatomy

- A container holding an icon, a concise message, and optional actions (buttons or links). Distinct colours indicate the message type — informational, warning, success, or error. Some banners include a close/dismiss button.
- 1. Background, 2. Step text, 3. Title

## Types

**Banner** — A prominent text area — step indicator plus a bold title — over a strong, bright gradient background, used to grab attention and convey progress or headings.

**Banner Base** — A background-only visual element — a curved gradient with no text, acting as a decorative foundation behind content or sections.

## Accessibility

- Clear, concise language and sufficient colour contrast (WCAG AA)
- Icons must not rely on colour alone — pair with descriptive text/labels
- Screen reader announcement via role="alert" (critical) or role="status" (less urgent)
- Dismiss buttons keyboard accessible and clearly labelled

{{> do-dont}}

## Content guidelines

**Be clear and concise** — Focus on the essential information users need to know or act on immediately.

**Use an appropriate tone** — Helpful and positive for informational/success; clear but calm for warnings/errors — never alarming.

**Prioritise key information first** — Users should understand the purpose at a glance.

**Guide users toward action when needed** — Use specific, action-oriented language ("Update Now") not vague terms ("Click Here").

**Keep messages relevant and timely** — Remove or update banners as soon as the information changes.

**Support accessibility** — Icons and text labels must always work together to communicate the message.

**Avoid overuse** — Too many banners cause users to start ignoring them.
