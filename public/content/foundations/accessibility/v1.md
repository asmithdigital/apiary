Designing for accessibility means creating inclusive experiences that everyone can use — regardless of ability.

Accessibility ensures that people with diverse needs can perceive, understand, navigate, and interact with our products. It's not a separate layer — it's a core part of how we design and build digital experiences.

## Why accessibility matters

Digital accessibility improves usability for all users — including those with vision, hearing, motor, or cognitive challenges. It also helps us meet legal standards, align with our brand values, and reduce rework by baking in best practices early.

- Build more inclusive products
- Reduce support issues and user frustration
- Improve search engine visibility and performance
- Support scalability across devices and platforms

## Core principles (WCAG)

- Perceivable — ensure users can perceive content through clear contrast, text alternatives, and adaptable layouts
- Operable — make interfaces usable by different input methods (keyboard, assistive tech) and avoid elements that cause physical reactions
- Understandable — provide predictable, readable, and helpful designs that guide users and reduce confusion
- Robust — build content that works reliably across current and future technologies and devices

## Accessibility in Apiary

Accessibility isn't an add-on — it's embedded directly into components and patterns. Wherever possible, accessible behaviours and markup are built into:

- Component specifications
- Design documentation
- Developer handoffs

_As Apiary evolves, we will continue enhancing accessibility coverage — adding contrast standards, keyboard behaviours, ARIA roles, and more._
